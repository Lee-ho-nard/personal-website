Intro

I'm based in Zürich, studying Economics & Law in the immersive program at Kantonsschule Limmattal. Most of what I actually care about, I've had to build for myself outside of school — how to write real code, how to get a product in front of strangers, how to turn an idea into something someone would use.

How I Got Here

I started trying out small businesses around age 12, long before any of it looked like a real skill — mostly failed ideas, small experiments, things that never went anywhere. What stuck wasn't any one of them, it was the habit: if something looked interesting or broken, try to build or fix it yourself instead of just watching.

That habit eventually turned into a self-directed curriculum — working through core economics, financial thinking, and market strategy on my own, then a structured reading list across sales, storytelling, and leadership — because I'd rather build the foundation myself than wait for a class to get to it.

Languages

I speak German, English, and French, and I'm learning Mandarin — through daily practice with Duolingo, Pleco, and Anki. Four languages isn't just a skill tag for me; it's the same logic as the small businesses: if something's worth having, it's worth building the habit for, one rep at a time.

Training

Outside of building, I stay active through running and the gym — training regularly, five to six times a week, working toward a half-marathon. I also play tennis and padel when I get the chance. None of it is really about the sport itself; it's the same discipline that shows up in building something every day whether or not anyone's watching.

What's Next

Looking ahead, I want to test what I'm building against the outside world — entrepreneurship programs, competitions, and eventually certificates and a wider network, once I've got something real enough behind me to make that conversation worth having.
